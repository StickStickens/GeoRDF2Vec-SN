# BGS+: main outcomes

Full evidence is in [notebooks/bgs_eda.ipynb](../notebooks/bgs_eda.ipynb). It mirrors the [dmg777k analysis](dmg777k_findings.md).

- **The task:** tell rock units laid down by rivers (FLUV, 93) from those laid down by ice (GLACI, 53), across 146 labelled units of the British Geological Survey's Lexicon. The graph has 916,199 triples and 274k nodes, about the size of dmg777k. The labels are few, so methods must be compared with repeated cross-validation, not one split.
- **Location is a strong signal here, unlike in dmg777k.** Glacial deposits lie in the north: all 11 units north of 56°N are GLACI, and 38 of 40 south of 52°N are FLUV. Coordinates of a unit's reference site alone reach macro F1 0.87, against 0.47 with shuffled labels. In dmg777k the same test gave 0.26 against 0.13.
- **The labelled units have no geometry of their own.** 97 inherit one from their stratotype, the reference site where the unit is defined. The other 49 inherit every geometry through the `skos:Concept` class hub. They all end up at one point in southern Scotland, as do 50.3% of all nodes (18.5% in dmg777k).
- **Relation-aware flooding measurably helps, before any training.** Dropping schema and attribute relations (types, schemes, statuses, ages, colours) removes that point entirely. For units whose stratotype is hidden, the median location error falls from 389 km to 59 km. The location-only classifier rises from macro F1 0.72 to 0.78. Dropping hubs by degree alone does not improve the classifier, so the relation choice matters.
- **Without reprojection the repo silently does nothing geographic.** Coordinates are British National Grid metres. The repo reads them as degrees, geopy rejects them, the error is caught and every distance becomes 0. [bgs_data_prep.py](../data_preparation/bgs_data_prep.py) converts them to WGS84.
- **Walks:** followed forwards, they stop after about 1.7 of 8 steps, as in dmg777k. Followed both ways, they always reach 8 steps and keep drifting further from the start, so walk length matters on this graph. The weights keep them 28–53 km closer to the start. 91–97% of such walks touch a national-centroid hub, though.
- **The graph explains much of the class on its own.** Parent units (`broader`), ages and some repeated description texts are pure for one class in training, which fits the published RDF2Vec accuracy of about 0.87 on BGS. Geography has to add to that, not replace it.
